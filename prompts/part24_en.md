PART 24: SUMMARY JUDGMENT
24.1. Scope of this Part
(1) This Part sets out the procedure by which the court may decide a claim or a particular issue without a trial.
(2) The court may give summary judgment against a claimant or a defendant in any type of court proceedings.
24.2. Grounds for giving summary judgment
(1) The court may give summary judgment against a claimant or a defendant on the whole of a claim or on a particular issue if:
(a) it considers that:
(i) the claimant has no real prospect of success on the claim or the issue; or
(ii) the defendant has no real prospect of a successful defence of the claim or the issue; and
(b) there is no other compelling reason why the case or the issue ought to be decided at a trial.
24.3. Procedure
(1) A claimant may not apply for summary judgment until the defendant against whom the application is made has filed a notice of appearance, unless the court gives permission.
(2) If a claimant applies for summary judgment before the defendant against whom the application is made files a defence, the defendant need not file a defence before the hearing.
(3) Where the hearing of an application for summary judgment is fixed, the respondent (or the parties where the hearing is fixed by the court of its own initiative) shall be given at least 14 days' notice of the date of the hearing.
24.4. The Application
(1) Subject to the provisions of this Part, the application shall be made in accordance with Part 23.
(2) The application or the evidence which is contained in it or referred to in it or served with it:
(a) shall identify concisely any legal point or provision in a document on which the applicant relies, and/or
(b) shall state that it is made because the applicant believes that, on the basis of the evidence, the respondent has no real prospect of success on the claim or the issue or (as the case may be) prospect of a successful defence of the claim and in either case shall state that the applicant does not know of any other reason why the claim or the issue ought to be tried.
(3) Unless the application itself contains the whole of the evidence (if any) on which the applicant relies, the application must identify the written evidence on which the applicant relies. This does not affect the right of the applicant to file additional evidence under rule 24.5(2).
24.5. Evidence for the purposes of the hearing of an application for summary judgment
(1) If the respondent to an application for summary judgment wishes to rely on written evidence at the hearing, the respondent shall:
(a) file the written evidence; and
(b) serve a copy of it on every other party to the application, at least 7 days before the hearing of the application for summary judgment, unless the court gives different directions.
(2) If the applicant wishes to rely on written evidence in reply, the applicant shall:
(a) file the written evidence; and
(b) serve a copy of it on the respondent, at least 3 days before the hearing of the application for summary judgment, unless the court gives different directions.
(3) Where the hearing of an application for summary judgment is fixed by the court of its own initiative without the issue of other directions:
(a) any party who wishes to rely on written evidence at the hearing:
(i) shall file the written evidence; and
(ii) unless the court orders otherwise, shall serve copies of it on every other party to the proceedings, at least 7 days before the date of the hearing;
(b) any party who wishes to rely on written evidence at the hearing in reply to written evidence of any other party:
(i) shall file the written evidence in reply; and
(ii) unless the court orders otherwise, shall serve copies of it on every other party to the proceedings, at least 3 days before the date of the hearing.
(4) This rule does not require:
(a) the filing of written evidence if it has already been filed; or
(b) the service of written evidence on a party on whom it has already been served.
(5) The whole of the written evidence which is filed shall be verified by a statement of truth.
24.6. Orders which the court may make
(1) The orders which the court may make upon an application under Part 24 include:
(a) judgment on the claim,
(b) striking out or dismissal of the claim,
(c) dismissal of the application,
(d) a conditional order.
(2) A conditional order is an order which requires a party:
(a) to pay a sum of money into court, or
(b) to take a particular step in relation to the claim or defence of the party, as the case may be, and provides that the claim of that party will be dismissed or the pleading of that party will be struck out if the party does not comply.
(3) The court may also:
(a) give directions as to the filing and service of a defence;
(b) give further directions for case management.
24.7. Setting aside of a summary judgment order
(1) If an order for summary judgment is made against a respondent who did not appear at the hearing of the application, the respondent may apply for the setting aside or variation of the order.
(2) At the hearing of an application under this rule, the court may make any order it considers just.

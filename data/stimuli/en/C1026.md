The Claimant Applicant (hereinafter "the Applicant") claims from the Defendant Respondent (hereinafter "the Respondent") the sum of €15.000, as owed by virtue of breach of an agreement for the sale of a vehicle or of unjust enrichment. In general terms it pleads that the Applicant contracted with the Respondent for the purchase of a specific vehicle and that, despite the payment of part of the consideration, namely €20.000, the vehicle was never delivered. The Respondent returned to the Applicant €5.000 out of the total of €20.000, thereby in substance accepting its debt, always according to the allegations of the Applicant.





By the application under consideration the Applicant seeks the issue of summary judgment on the whole of the claim, since in its position there is no real prospect of a successful defence and there is no other compelling reason why the case ought to be decided at a trial.





The Application is supported by an affidavit of the Applicant's director [name of applicant's director]. He alleges that the oral contract of the parties, dated 6/2/2024, provided for the sale of a specific vehicle to the Applicant by the Respondent, in consideration of the payment of the sum of €50.000. On 30/3/2024, on account of the failure to perform the terms of the original agreement for the sale of the vehicle in good time on the part of the Respondent, the parties drew up a supplementary written agreement. In that agreement, Exhibit 2 to the affidavit of [name of applicant's director], it is provided that the director of the Respondent would travel to England in order to take all the necessary measures for the delivery of the vehicle to the Applicant, at the latest by 15/4/2024, free of any taxes and customs clearance expenses in the Republic of Cyprus. The Applicant on 6 and 11/3/2024 delivered €10.000 on each occasion to the Respondent, but the vehicle was not delivered.





As Exhibit 5 [name of applicant's director] attached his written messages with the director of the Respondent. There [name of respondent's director] states that he will return the sum of €20.000 to the Applicant, although he complains that [name of applicant's director] had spoken directly with the supplier of the vehicle and for that reason "the deal fell through", the transport of the vehicle was frustrated. Finally, on the following day [name of respondent's director] stated that he is waiting for the sums which he had paid to a company in England to be remitted to him and then to pay the Applicant. On 10/5/2024 the Respondent returned to the Applicant by way of a bank cheque the sum of €5.000 (Exhibit 6). On Exhibit 6 the following transaction details are recorded: "Refund amound (5000) out of (20000) for cancellation of the car purchase [vehicle registration number]".





Subsequently and since the remaining sum was not paid, letters were exchanged between the advocates of the parties, in which there are in substance reflected the positions which were developed also in the documents which were filed with the Court, pleadings and affidavits. The position of [name of applicant's director] is that the allegations of the Respondent constitute afterthoughts and an attempt to avoid its responsibilities. He maintains in addition that the Respondent is estopped from denying its obligation to pay the sum owed, since it in substance acknowledged it through Exhibit 6, while he refers also to the impossibility of service on the Respondent, with the purpose of delaying the procedure.





The Respondent raises a series of grounds of opposition. The principal grounds, as they were finally promoted, are the following: the Applicant does not come with clean hands, the legal and factual issues which are raised do not permit the success of the application, while it is further raised that the Applicant has no cause of action against the Respondent and that the legal basis is not specific as the case law requires.





In support of the grounds of opposition an affidavit of the director of the Respondent was filed. [name of respondent's director] admits both the conclusion of the oral contract of the parties and the payment of the sum of €20.000 by the Applicant to the Respondent. He explains that the vehicle would be purchased from the English company [name of company], to which he paid the sum of 32,000 English Pounds. The Respondent, that is to say, paid not only the sum which was given to it by the Applicant but also a further sum, which it borrowed (Exhibit 5). The delivery of the car had thus been arranged with an expected day of arrival of 14/4/2024 (Exhibit 7). After intervention by the Applicant, however, the dispatch of the vehicle did not prove possible, because it was judged that the VAT would not be refunded and [name of company] would lose money. Further actions of [name of applicant's director] frustrated the dispatch of the vehicle, while [name of company] promised [name of respondent's director] that it would return the money which the Respondent paid and did not do so (see Exhibits 11 A and B).








Finally the Applicant purchased another vehicle from [name of company], corresponding to that which the Respondent had ordered from the same English company, bypassing the Respondent (Exhibit 13 invoice dated 24/4/2024). And it used the money which was paid by the Respondent as a credit balance for the purchase directly from [name of company].





The position of the Respondent is that it would return the money to the Applicant, provided that it received it back from [name of company], especially from the moment the Applicant had contracted with the said English company. Finally it yielded to pressure from [name of applicant's director], after intimidation and threats, paying him the sum of €5.000. The Respondent maintains that it lost money from the Applicant and [name of company], since the Respondent paid a sum which was finally used as payment towards the price for the purchase of a vehicle from [name of company].

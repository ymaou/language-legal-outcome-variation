The Applicant seeks the issue of summary judgment for the following prayers for relief:


"( A.) Judgment against the Defendant for the sum of €4.000,00 cent, on the basis of breach and/or by virtue of a written and / or oral agreement and / or unlawful possession of immovable property and/or unlawful trespass and/or breach of agreement as damages for breach of contract and / or as the agreed price and / or of an acknowledged and / or liquidated debt and / or by virtue of unlawful possession of immovable property and / or unlawful trespass as damages and/or otherwise and/or as rental value and/or loss of use or / and loss of rents and intermediate rents of €400 monthly until the resolution of the problem out of the ground-floor premises and/or immovable property and/or house on the street [location].


( B.) Alternatively to ( A) above, judgment against the Defendant for the sum which is stated in ( A) above, as damages and/or otherwise and/or equal to the rental value of the immovable property and/or loss of use or / and loss of rents and intermediate rents of €950 monthly until the resolution of the problem out of the ground-floor premises and/or immovable property and/or house on the street [location].


( C.) Judgment against the Defendant for eviction or / and delivery of the free and vacant possession of the ground-floor premises and/or immovable property and/or house on the street [location]"


After the filing of the pleadings and of a questionnaire by the Defendant, the Claimant chose to file the application under consideration, before filing a Questionnaire.


By an affidavit of his the Claimant maintains that he is the lawful owner of the disputed immovable property, as it is described in the above prayers for relief (Exhibit 1). In the disputed premises there resided until his death on 28/02/2013 the father of the Claimant, being the owner of the disputed immovable property, and it was not rented out nor offered for rent before his death. To that end he attached a utility bill as Exhibit 2. The parties signed a tenancy agreement of of the disputed immovable property on 27/12/2018, without the signature of two witnesses. The agreement provided that upon the expiry of the Tenancy Agreement the Tenant loses any tenancy rights and must deliver the premises in good condition, as he had received it.


After the expiry of the tenancy agreement, on 31/12/2020, the Defendant continued to reside in the immovable property, paying the same sum as rent. And this despite the fact that in the agreement of the parties it was provided that the Tenant may extend the tenancy for one further period with an increase of rent which may be agreed between the tenant and the owner. After the expiry of the period the Claimant states that he notified the Defendant that he possesses the disputed immovable property unlawfully, arbitrarily and wilfully and called upon him to deliver it, however with no result.


On 3/2/2024 the advocates of the Claimant sent a letter of termination to the Defendant and asked him to deliver the disputed immovable property to them within one month (Exhibit 4). The Defendant did not vacate the immovable property and so the Claimant appointed an expert, a valuer, who calculated the market rent at 950 euro. Subsequently a letter was sent as a Pre-Action Protocol to the Defendant. The Claimant maintains that the existence of a defence of the Defendant does not emerge. He states that: "the Defendant through his Defence does not establish any material issue for trial and there is no dispute in relation to any factual or legal issues which raise any reasonable doubt as to whether I am entitled to judgment."


The Defendant by his grounds of opposition counters that he has a real and substantial prospect of a successful defence, since he advances specific allegations and factual circumstances which require examination by the Court. He places before the Court material issues both as to the actual facts and as to their legal evaluation, which are clearly disputed and cannot be decided without the proper investigation through a full trial. There is a compelling reason for which the dispute at issue ought to be tried in the normal way. Finally he disputes factual and legal issues which raise reasonable doubt as to whether the Claimant is entitled to judgment.


The Defendant by his affidavit, through which he supports the grounds of opposition, puts forward as his main position that the District Court of [location] lacks jurisdiction to deal with the present action and that exclusive jurisdiction for the resolution of the dispute at issue lies with the Rent Control Court of [location]. To that end he argues, stating that he is a statutory tenant of the disputed immovable property, since he rented it for the first time in 2015 and for that purpose he signed with the Claimant a Tenancy Agreement for the period from 1/7/2015 until 30/6/2017 and rent of €500 per month. Also that "When the first tenancy agreement expired, he continued to reside within the disputed dwelling with my wife and his three minor children and paid to the Claimant the agreed rent of €500 until the end of 2018." On 27/12/2018 he signed with the Claimant a second Tenancy Agreement for the period from 1/1/2019 until 31/12/2020. Despite its expiry he continues to possess the ground-floor dwelling as a statutory tenant, as the Claimant always alleges, and to pay unfailingly to the Claimant the agreed rent of €550 each month.


It is the position of the Defendant that the ground-floor dwelling was always rented out or for rent, even before he himself rented it. It was rented to students for approximately two years and before the students, from time to time it was rented to other persons, whom he names, as third parties conveyed to him. The father of the Claimant and his domestic helper resided, until the death of the former, in a basement studio, beneath the disputed immovable property.


The Defendant further maintains that in term 6 of the Tenancy Agreement dated 27/12/2018 it is stated that: "It is agreed that the increase on the rent shall be paid in accordance with the Rent Control Law of the Republic of Cyprus", a fact which according to him demonstrates that he was a statutory tenant of the disputed immovable property. Further it is admitted that the said agreement is not signed by two witnesses.


The Defendant alleges that he proposed a specific increase of the rent to the Claimant, but the latter did not accept it. And he rejects the valuation which the Claimant attaches to his affidavit, because the valuer never entered within the disputed house and never inspected the condition in which it is. Both the photographs attached to the report and the references in it concern previous years, when a valuation had to be made for borrowing and other purposes of the Claimant. He explains also that he replied through his advocates to the letter of the advocates of the Claimant for the termination of the contractual relationship between them.

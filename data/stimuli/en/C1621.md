By the Application summary judgment is sought against the Defendant as per paragraph A of the prayer for relief of the Statement of Claim, which in substance seeks the prohibition of the Defendant from filing and promoting an application for the winding up of the Claimant on the basis of the disputed debt which is referred to in the demand for payment of the Defendant against the Claimant dated 27/02/2025 (the "Payment Demand").





The filing of the Application under consideration was preceded by the trial of the interlocutory application of the Claimant dated 17/3/2025, in which the present Court by its decision made final the issue of an interim order, similar to the final order which is sought by the Claim.





The application is based on the Civil Procedure Rules of 2023 Parts 3, 23 and 24, on the Companies Law articles 209, 211 and 212, on the common law, on the principles of equity and on the inherent power of the Court.





The application is supported by the affidavit of [name of deponent], director of the Claimant who adopts, for the purposes of the present application, his three affidavits which supported the application for an interim Order. I summarise the evidence as it emerges from them: The Applicant is a registered Cypriot company of limited liability with issued share capital of €8.000.000. Its sole shareholder and parent company is [name of parent company] (with seat in the British Virgin Islands). It constitutes a member of the international construction Group [name of parent company], the net asset value (NAV) of which exceeds $200.000.000. The Applicant is a fully solvent and profitable undertaking. According to its recent audited financial statements (30/09/2024), its assets exceed its liabilities by €1,8 million, maintaining a strong capitalisation with the continuous support of the Group. The Respondent is also a Cypriot construction company, the financial condition of which is widely known in the market as precarious, being unable to meet its obligations. Recently, its contracting contracts were terminated by employers on account of inability to perform (indicatively: the project at [location], Phase A' and the Municipal Market [location]). In December of the year 2020, the Applicant and the Respondent established a general partnership/joint venture with the name "[name of defendant company] - [name of applicant company] JV" (the "Joint Venture"), with the purpose of the execution of public and private projects. For each project a similar type of agreement was signed (Joint Venture Agreement), which specified percentages of participation (30%-70%), obligations of financing of working capital and proportionate allocation of profits/losses. In parallel the parties had also concluded autonomous, non-joint-venture contracts (Subcontract, Cooperation Agreement).





The present dispute springs from the project "Centre of Business Innovation - Restoration and Extension of the Old Municipal Market" of [name of municipality] (the "Project"). Although the construction contract was signed in the name of the Defendant (on account of a prior award of tender), by virtue of a relevant Joint Venture Agreement (11/01/2021), it was expressly agreed that the Project belongs internally to the Joint Venture, with percentages of participation of 60% for the Defendant and 40% for the Claimant. On 27/02/2025, the Defendant served on the Claimant a letter of demand by virtue of Article 212(a) of Cap. 113, claiming the sum of €639,888.47 as the 40% of the allegedly total loss of the Project (€1.599.721,18), threatening with the filing of a winding-up petition in the event of non-compliance within 21 days (that is by 20/03/2025). The Claimant rejects the demand as entirely unfounded, contrasting the following financial data: While [name of municipality] was paying the Defendant, the latter, in breach of a relevant undertaking of its own (dated 02/02/2022), was not rendering the sums to the Joint Venture. Specifically, it collected €4.738.031,15, rendered a mere €1.654.472,02 and unlawfully withheld €3.083.559,39. Further, the Joint Venture paid Project expenses in the amount of €3.729.486, with the sum of the net financing amounting to €2.075.030. The Defendant did not contribute anything and the Claimant covered this sum entirely. On the basis of the expenses which the Defendant itself alleges that it paid (€2.731.442,52) in relation to the money which it withheld, it is in a positive cash flow (surplus of €352.116,87). According to term 11.1 of the agreement, the allocation of losses is made after the financings are taken into account. Consequently, the Defendant owes the Claimant the share of the loss which corresponds to it (€959.832,71) plus the corresponding sum from the surplus which it withheld (€352.116,87). The Defendant owes in addition millions to the Claimant on account of systematic refusal of financing of the Joint Venture (debt of €4.520.714 on the basis of financial statements of 2024, plus subsequent contributions). It also owes €307.647,99 by virtue of a separate Subcontract (for which the Claimant has already served a demand on the basis of article 212(a) since 23/01/2025) and €49.246,87 by virtue of a Cooperation Agreement.





The debt is disputed in good faith and substantially. The Payment Demand on the part of the Defendant constitutes an abuse of procedure, with the sole purpose of the exercise of improper pressure so that the Claimant does not promote its own lawful claims of millions. If the filing of a winding-up petition against the Claimant is permitted, the damage will be incapable of assessment in money and catastrophic: Its bank accounts will immediately be frozen, paralysing its daily construction activity (payments of salaries, subcontractors, suppliers). On the basis of Term 63.1 of the Public Contracts, the contracting authorities acquire an automatic right of termination of the existing contracts of the Claimant for public works, simply and solely upon the commencement of a winding-up procedure (irrespective of whether it is well founded). There will be irreparably harmed the commercial credit, the reputation and the creditworthiness both of the Claimant and of the entire Group, causing chain negative effects (domino phenomenon).





On the basis of the above evidence, as he states, it is obvious that the Claimant disputes, with foundation and in good faith, the alleged debt which constitutes the object of the Payment Demand, and that the Defendant is not a creditor of the Claimant. Further, the Payment Demand was not served at the registered office of the Claimant. Consequently, in the event that a winding-up petition were filed by the Defendant on the basis of the Payment Demand it would be doomed to failure and, as such, would constitute an abuse of the judicial procedure. Therefore, it is right and just according to their position that a final order be issued prohibiting the Defendant from filing and promoting an application for the winding up of the Claimant on the basis of the Payment Demand. There is no defence whatsoever either as a matter of facts or as a matter of law to the Claim of the Claimant.





In addition, the issue of the abusiveness of a possible winding-up petition of the Claimant by the Defendant on the basis of the Payment Demand has already been decided in favour of the Claimant within the framework of the interlocutory application dated 17/3/25, and there is no ground for something different to be decided at a final stage. Further, the Defendant has no real prospect of successful defence of the Claim, and there is no other compelling reason whatsoever for which the case or the issue ought to be decided at a trial. If the Claim were tried after a full hearing, this would entail a pointless waste of judicial resources and time, as well as of legal costs, since its outcome is predetermined. It is their position that, on the basis of the evidence, the Defendant has no prospect of successful defence of the Claim, and they know of no other reason for which the Claim ought to be tried.





The Respondent filed an opposition to the application. The grounds of opposition are, verbatim, the following (the spelling and syntax are retained):


1. The Application under consideration was filed in a blackmailing manner and/or in order to exercise pressure and/or to harass and/or blackmail the Respondent and/or the Applicant abuses the judicial procedure (abuse of process) for an extraneous purpose.


2. The lawful preconditions for the issue of Summary Judgment by virtue of Part 24 of the Civil Procedure Rules 2023 do not exist.



5. The summary procedure does not constitute a suitable means for the examination of complex accounting and financial issues which require the evidence of an expert and/or a detailed examination of the accounts of the joint venture.


6. The order sought (anti-suit) which prevents the Respondent from the bringing or continuation of legal proceedings is contrary to Article 30(1) and (3) of the Constitution of the Republic of Cyprus and to Article 6 of the ECHR, since it violates the fundamental right of access to Justice.


7. The pleadings themselves and/or financial statements and/or other documents of the Applicant recognise/admit a debt in the amount of €621,476 to the Respondent, a fact which undermines the very application for Summary Judgment.


8. The Applicant is insolvent and/or is unable to pay its debts and/or is a separate legal entity from other companies of its group.


9. There is available to the Applicant a validation order by virtue of the Companies Law Cap. 113, which constitutes a sufficient remedy for any concern of the Applicant in relation to transfers of assets or other acts of management during pendency of proceedings, rendering unnecessary the order which is sought.


10. The interlocutory order was issued with different criteria and a lower evidential route, and its being made final and/or its issue as summary judgment cannot be based on the same bar.





The opposition is supported by an affidavit of the Managing Director of [name of defendant company] (Defendant-Respondent), who declares that he is fully authorised by the Respondent to make the affidavit.





He knows, as he states, personally the facts of the present case, inasmuch as he is the person who handles exclusively on the part of the Defendant all the issues which concern the Joint Venture Contract at issue and the obligations deriving from it. Where he refers to legal issues, he has taken the advice of their legal advisers.





He denies that the Defendant has no defence in the present case. On the contrary, it maintains a serious, substantial, bona fide and credible defence, which is founded on documents, accounting data and on admissions of the Claimant itself which overturn the core of the application.





He adopts, for the purposes of the present, an affidavit of his dated 12.05.2025 which was filed in support of the opposition to the application for an interlocutory order, together with the Exhibits which accompany it, the Supplementary Affidavit of his dated 30/6/2025 as well as the Defence with the Preliminary Objections. The evidence which the Respondent offered within the framework of the application for an interlocutory Order is summarised as follows: The Applicants attempted to mislead the Court, presenting their relationship as a simple relationship of "Contractor - Subcontractor", while in reality it is a matter of a Joint Venture. The Subcontract Agreement (16.07.2021) was made purely for "accounting order" and so that the presence of the Applicants at the site of [name of municipality] would be legitimised (since the Municipality had contracted only with the Respondent). From February of 2023, the Applicants had full financial control of the accounts of the Joint Venture. They paid €2,5 million to third subcontractors, but deliberately never paid themselves for 2-3 years, something which proves that they did not consider themselves real subcontractors. The Respondent alleges that the Applicants owe it money and not the opposite. On the basis of the Joint Venture Agreement, the Applicants bear 40% of the losses and the Respondent 60%. The project had a loss of €1.599.721,18, therefore the Applicants owe immediately €639.888,47 to the Respondent (for which the Notice of Demand was sent). The Respondent admits that it has an accounting debt to the Joint Venture, but on the basis of the agreement dated 12.05.2023, this debt is not immediately payable (it will be cleared at the final financial closing of the project and will be set off against future profits). The financial statements of 2023 and 2024 present apparently losses because the Applicants changed arbitrarily the accounting practice, omitting to include non-certified revenues and future claims from the Employer (Municipality).





The filing of a winding-up petition is a lawful right of the creditor. If the accounts of the Applicants are frozen, there exists the legal remedy of the Validation Order (Order of Validation of Transactions) from the Court so that the company may continue to operate. The risk of the Applicants losing the public works is baseless, because the contracting parties with the public are the Joint Ventures (separate legal persons) and not the Applicants personally. The Respondent sent lawfully the notice on the basis of Article 212(a) of Cap. 113. There is no unlawful threat whatsoever. The negative publicity or the concern in the market constitutes a natural commercial risk and not "legally irreparable damage". The order which the Applicants obtained prevents the Respondent from having recourse to justice since it flagrantly violates Article 30 of the Constitution and Article 6 of the ECHR. The Court cannot in a civil action "pre-decide" whether a future winding-up petition will be abusive.





According to the deponent, the Defendant has filed a Defence in which it raises 4 preliminary objections, that the Cypriot Courts have no power of issue of anti-suit injunctions since such orders are contrary to Articles 30(1) and 30(3) of the Constitution and to Article 6 of the ECHR, that the Court cannot pronounce on preconditions for the issue of a winding-up order within the framework of a full action, that Claimant has no cause of action which would justify the present claim and that the claim is indefinite, unclear and lacks legal documentation.





It is their position that the remedy sought, that is a final order preventing the Defendant from the exercise of its right to submit a winding-up petition, constitutes an anti-suit injunction and that as a result, even if the present application otherwise satisfied the criteria of summary judgment, the remedy which is sought is of necessity impermissible since the final deprivation of a fundamental constitutional right of winding up, without a full trial, is contrary to the Constitution and to the ECHR and cannot be issued by way of summary procedure. Further, that in the event in which a winding-up petition were filed, there is provided by the Companies Law, Cap. 113 the possibility of obtaining an order which permits the carrying out of banking transactions and the management of the company during the pendency of proceedings on the basis of which every allegation of irreparable harm to the Claimant is entirely overturned.





It is their position that the criteria for the issue of summary judgment are not satisfied in the present case, where facts are extensively disputed, accounting expert opinion is required, the credibility of the witnesses is central to the outcome, and the Applicant itself admits in its pleadings that it owes the sum claimed provided that the data are correct. Of decisive importance, and entirely passed over in silence by the Applicant in the application and in the witness statement which supports it, is the fact that the Applicant itself in its Statement of Claim (par. 28) expressly recognises that "the Project was loss-making, and that the total loss exceeds €1 m. If the sums which are stated in Annex A of the Payment Demand are correct, there emerges a loss in the amount of €1.599.721,18, and indeed the loss which corresponds to the Claimant amounts to €639.888,47." According to their position the above admission strikes at the application for summary judgment since the Applicant admits that the Project was indeed loss-making and therefore the claim of the Defendant is not invented or fanciful, that if the financial data are correct, the loss which to the Applicant would be €639.888,47 (that is the sum which the Respondent seeks) and that its sole dispute concerns the accuracy of the financial data, an issue which inevitably requires a full hearing.





In addition, as he states, the Applicants through the ΕΔ ΘΚ confirm that they owe a sum in excess of €620.000 to the Respondent for the Innovation project. The Applicants expressly admit in the said paragraph that for the year ending 30.09.2023 they owe the sum of €288.813, while for the year ending 30.09.2024 they admit that they owe the sum of €149.197. Therefore, if it is taken into account that in the minutes dated 19.04.2023, which form part of the Agreement dated 12.05.2023, the Applicants admit their debt for the sum of €183.466 for that year, in substance, the Applicants admit that they owe the sum of €621.476, for the project. That is, a sum which has no substantial difference from the sum of the notice of demand, which the Respondent claims.





It is also advanced by the deponent that the central point of the present dispute lies in the accounts of the Joint Venture in the Project "Centre of Business Innovation - Restoration of the Old Municipal Market" of [name of municipality] (the "Project"). These accounts are extensively disputed as to the total volume of revenues which were collected on account of the Joint Venture but were never rendered to it, the correct characterisation of expenses, the non-rendering of revenues in the amount of about €3.000.000 which the Applicant itself expressly recognises (par. 26 of the Statement of Claim) and the final debit-credit balance of each party. These issues require detailed analysis of accounting data, examination of bank movements, contracting contracts, invoices, correspondence and expert opinion from an independent expert. The requirement for expert opinion on its own constitutes a compelling reason for the application to be dismissed.





He points out that the Applicant, in its Statement of Claim (par. 27), recognises that the Joint Venture collected revenues in the amount of €1.654.456, while it paid out expenses in the amount of €3.729.486, that is it financed the Project by €2.075.030, a sum and or financing which emerges from the statements of the Applicant itself that it must be rendered to the Joint Venture while the project itself presented by common admission of the Applicant and of the Defendant a loss in excess of €1,500,000 and as a result the need for financing of the Project ought to have been expected and understandable. On the contrary, the Applicant presents the said fact as a responsibility or omission of the Defendant. This clearing has not been done, and in it there certainly emerges serious dispute, and as a result no final balance can be calculated at this stage. Further, he points out the fact that from February of 2023, following agreement of the parties, the full control and the management of all the bank accounts of the Joint Venture ([name of bank]) was transferred exclusively to the representatives of the Applicant. This means that for a period of more than two years, the Applicant had absolute and exclusive control of the money flows of the Joint Venture. He notes that the relevant document (Mandate for Partnership Account / Alteration of Signatories dated 24.02.2023) together with the minute dated 22.02.2023 which constitute a decisive document of regulation or indeed limitation of the responsibilities of the opposing parties was never made known by the Applicant to the Court during the application for an interlocutory order.





He says that during the period during which the Applicant held full control of the account, the Joint Venture paid to the other subcontractors of the Project a total sum of €2.555.627,26. Nevertheless, the Applicant never paid itself the remaining sum for the allegedly "unpaid services" which it says that it provided as a "subcontractor". The explanation is obvious: the relationship was not that of contractor-subcontractor but a joint venture one, and as a party to a joint venture, the Applicant is not entitled to direct payment from the account, but to an allocation of profits/losses. From this it emerges that no lawful reason in principle exists for the filing of the present application. Further, according to the same document of the agreement of the parties dated 22/2/2023, a contribution to the bank account for a sum in excess of €1.000.000 is not contemplated, a limitation which the Applicant violated to a multiple degree. Also, according again to the agreement itself, it was agreed that the Applicant would indemnify the Respondent for any loss or damage which would emerge exclusively from the operation of the accounts of the Joint Venture.





He adds that the Applicant financed the Joint Venture in part through funds which come from other companies of its group which are situated abroad. This fact does not prove, its financial strength or solvency. On the contrary, it proves exactly the opposite, that, the Applicant itself, as a separate legal entity, did not have of its own the necessary funds and was forced to rely on financing from another company of the group. The dependence of the Applicant on funds of a company of the group does not confirm its financial sufficiency. On the contrary, it brings out its inability as an autonomous legal entity to meet the contractual obligations which it undertook towards the Joint Venture. The funds of another company of the group do not belong to the Applicant, do not constitute its own assets, and cannot be evaluated as evidence of its own solvency, with the result that there emerges, in the absence of other data, that the Applicant is not in a position to pay its debts.





Beyond the above he submits that the nature of the final order sought which prevents the Defendant from the exercise of its lawful right to submit a winding-up petition, does not permit summary trial. It constitutes a final remedy which presupposes a full and final judgment on the substantive rights of the parties. It is contrary to the nature and the purpose of summary trial, which aims at the settlement of cases without substantial dispute not at the final deprivation of lawful rights without a full hearing. The winding-up petition before a Cypriot Court constitutes a lawful right of every creditor on the basis of the Companies Law. In substance, the permanent suspension of the right of winding up proceeds only if the court diagnoses, after a full hearing, that the debt is nil or unfounded to a degree that the submission of a winding-up petition would constitute an abuse of procedure and/or that there is no real dispute whatsoever which would justify judicial examination. Neither of the two applies in the present instance.





It is his position that for the submission of a winding-up petition to be judged an abuse of procedure, the dispute of the debt must be material, in good faith and with an evidential background, and must relate to the very existence and the legal basis of the debt, not simply to its amount. A dispute only of the sum does not suffice for the deprivation of the lawful right of filing a winding-up petition. In the present instance, the Applicant expressly recognises in its pleadings (par. 28 of the Statement of Claim) that "if the sums... are correct, the loss which corresponds to the Claimant amounts to €639.888,47". This is not a dispute of the debt, it is a dispute of the numerical data. The legal basis of the Joint Venture, the execution of the Project, its loss-making nature and the obligation of allocation of losses are, he considers, admitted. Therefore, the debt exists simply its exact amount is disputed. In these circumstances, the Defendant, as a creditor of the Joint Venture, was and is absolutely justified in proceeding to a Payment Demand by virtue of article 212(a) of Cap. 113. This act does not constitute an abuse but constitutes the exercise of a lawful right.





As the deponent also submits the interlocutory decision dated 14/11/2025 was issued on the basis of an entirely different legal criterion and does not bind the Court during the examination of the present application. Particularly important is that no final judgment whatsoever has been issued in relation to the substantive rights of the parties as also that the deponents have not been subjected to cross-examination nor was their credibility verified. In substance, the Applicant seeks to convert into a permanent remedy an interim order which was issued without a hearing, without examination of the evidence and without any final judgment on the substantive rights of the parties, and indeed by way of summary procedure.





Further, according to their position there are "compelling reasons" for which the case ought to be tried with a full procedure. Specifically, that accounting expert opinion is required for the checking, the verification and the calculation of the joint venture accounts, examination and cross-examination of witnesses as to the manner in which the Applicant managed the funds and the payments of the Joint Venture during exclusive control of the accounts. Further, the remedy sought is by nature final and irrevocable and if it is issued by summary judgment it would obstruct for ever the exercise of a lawful right, and there is pending Winding-up Petition No. [number of winding-up petition] before the D.C. [location], which touches on the same dispute and the present claim and the winding-up petition are directly interdependent. Finally, the non-disclosure of critical documents by the Applicant constitutes a self-standing compelling reason for a full hearing, so that the Court may evaluate the entire factual framework of the case
